Understanding Iron Nutrition for Women

Nutritional anemia is predominantly caused by insufficient bioavailable iron intake. Explore food sources, absorption enhancers, and dietary inhibitors.

Heme vs. Non-Heme Iron
Bioavailability Mechanism

Dietary iron exists in two distinct biochemical forms with fundamentally different absorption characteristics:

Heme Iron (Animal Sources)

Bound to hemoglobin and myoglobin proteins. Highly bioavailable (absorbed at 15% to 35% efficiency). It is absorbed intact via the heme carrier protein 1 (HCP1) and is largely unaffected by dietary inhibitors like tea or phytates.

Non-Heme Iron (Plant Sources)

Found in plants, grains, legumes, and fortified foods. Absorbed at a lower baseline rate of 2% to 20%. Highly sensitive to enhancers (such as Vitamin C) and inhibitors (tannins, calcium, phytates).

Vitamin C as an Iron Enhancer
Chemical Reduction (Fe3+ → Fe2+)

Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) is the most potent physiological promoter of non-heme iron absorption:

  • Chemical Reduction: Non-heme iron in food is mostly in the insoluble ferric form (Fe3+). Vitamin C reduces it to soluble ferrous iron (Fe2+), which binds readily to the divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) in the enterocyte membrane.
  • Chelation Protection: Vitamin C forms a soluble chelate complex at acidic gastric pH that remains soluble and absorbable even in the neutral pH environment of the small intestine.
  • Counteracting Phytates: Just 50 mg of Vitamin C in a meal can double or triple the absorption of iron from a grain-heavy dish.
Tip: Squeeze fresh lemon juice over cooked lentils or eat guava/citrus alongside your meals.

Major Dietary Iron Inhibitors

Consuming these compounds concurrently with iron-rich meals significantly reduces uptake efficiency:

Tea & Coffee (Tannins & Polyphenols)

Form insoluble chelates with non-heme iron, reducing absorption by up to 60-70%. Maintain an interval of 60 to 90 minutes between tea/coffee and iron-rich meals.

Phytates (Unsoaked Grains & Bran)

Found in whole grains, seeds, and bran. Soaking, sprouting, or fermenting pulses and grains significantly reduces phytate content.

Excess Calcium (Milk / Antacids)

Calcium competes with iron for binding pathways. Avoid consuming large glasses of milk or calcium antacids directly during an iron-focused meal.
